Mathletics for Home — Onboarding & Parent Dashboard

mathletics.com · Product Design · 6 months

View Figma Design

Mathletics for Home is a version of Mathletics built specifically for parents — whether they're looking to give their child extra support outside the classroom, help them catch up, or provide a different way of learning for kids who've struggled in a traditional school environment. Home schoolers are a significant part of the user base too. Parents buy the subscription and manage everything on behalf of their children. This was the project to make that experience actually work.

The problem

The sign-up flow required 15 steps before a child could log in for the first time. Across those steps, users were being routed through multiple external payment platforms, visibly leaving the site to complete their purchase before being brought back. It felt broken because it was.


For those who made it through, the problems didn't stop. Parents had no clear idea what to do after signing up. Many couldn't work out how to get their child's credentials set up or how to actually get them into the platform. The drop-off wasn't just at sign-up — it was right after it.


And underneath all of it, the free trial model wasn't converting. Getting the messaging right around commitment and expectations was as important as the flow itself.

The work

Reducing 15 steps to 6. Redesigned the onboarding flow end to end, stripping it back to six essential steps. This required rearchitecting how payment was handled — moving away from external routing and embedding the payment system directly within the page so the experience never felt like the user was leaving to go somewhere else.


Post sign-up clarity. Designed a clear next-steps experience immediately after purchase, giving parents a direct path to getting their child set up with the right credentials and logged in straight away. What had been the biggest source of confusion became one of the most resolved parts of the flow.


Commitment-based free trial. Repositioned the free trial model to require a credit card upfront. Designed the messaging carefully around this — clear, honest, and low-pressure — so that users understood exactly what they were committing to. Getting that tone right was as important as the interaction design itself.


Parent dashboard. Designed a dashboard giving parents a single view of all their children's learning. Add students, monitor progress, and manage the subscription from one place. Weekly performance breakdowns give parents a meaningful read on how their child is actually going, making the product feel valuable beyond just access.


Upgrade pathway. Built a clear route from monthly to annual subscription directly into the dashboard, surfacing the long-term value at the moment parents are most engaged with the product.

The impact

Sign-up reduced from 15 steps to 6. Parents could get their child into the platform immediately after purchase for the first time, resolving the single biggest post-sign-up drop-off point.


The embedded payment experience removed the friction of leaving the site entirely, creating a more trustworthy and complete feel to the purchase flow.


The redesign contributed to a measurable revenue increase on the B2C side of the business.
