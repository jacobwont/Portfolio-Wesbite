Mathletics for Home

Onboarding flow
Redesign

Overview

Mathletics is an online maths web-app designed to help children learn maths based on the local curriculum for their age and location.

Currently the majority of the students using Mathletics use access platform via a school subscription, however the app is also available for home users.

This project aimed to redesign the onboarding flow to improve the experience for parents and increase the sign-up and conversion rate.

 

Project Information

I worked on this project as the primary designer in both a UX and UI capacity for 4 months, alongside a Product Manager whilst consulting with the UX Lead.

This project leverages on a body on research I conducted for the Parent Dashboard Project which detailed identifies insights on parents who use Mathletics for Home.

Discovery

Project Needs

After talking with both marketing, customer service and development teams- I identified the following key goals, technical requirements and current issues.





Creating a benchmark

Building upon the issues had been perviously been identified by the marketing and customer service teams, I auidited the current designs before conducting some initial user testing to benchmark the current onboarding flow and better understand the user experience.

 

 

 

Usability Testing

I ran 4 quick usability test with parents not-yet familiar with Mathletics. I followed each of testing sessions with short interviews designed to learn more about parents perceptions of the process to signing up and log their child in to begin learning.

As expected - this research revealed many issues leading to a poor user experience.

 

Understanding the flow

After mapping the current flow I Identified 15 individual steps were needed to be completed before logging your child into the Student Centre.

 

After conducting this initial research, I understood that the process was currently broken and provided a huge opportunity to improve the overall experience of Mathletics for Home users.

This allowed me to create a problem statement to guide the re-design of the onboardining process

 

Problem Statement

Parents need a quick and easy way to sign up and their children started learning on Mathletics using any device.

Design

Re-designing the User Flow

Informed by previous research, I began to map out some revised flows which could drastically reduce the amount of creating two alternative flows. One which would take users right into the ‘Create Account’ page after clicking a ‘Free Trial ‘ button and the other which would allow the users to select their desired plan after clicking a ‘See Pricing’ Button.

These updated flows reduce the amount of step required by uses from from 15, down to 6 or 7.

Wire-framing for responsive design

After mapping out the user-flows I created wireframe to experiment with the layout across all screen sizes sbefore creating a mid-fidelity prototype to test both designed flows and the with internal stakeholders and the broader team.

 

 

Testing results

The testing affirmed the flow was logical and easy to follow. However, many stakeholders were confused about the free trial and felt it lacked prominence. I tabled the issues reported and addressed each of the concerns by severity.

Design Iterations

After the testing was completed I iterated upon the designs, to address the concerned raised. Below is a short summary of the more-significant updates.

Pricing card updates

The refined pricing cards moves the ‘Save 20%’ banner to the top of the screen to indicate cost advance of signing up two children at the same time before the user selects the number of children they wish to purchase subscriptions for.

Add children page updates

The second iteration of the design for the ‘Add children page’ was simplified, which included reducing the width of the main card and .moving the child pricing banner from the right to the left to improve the visual flow. The ‘Save 20%’ callout was moved to the bottom of the card to reduce the chance of distraction before the call to action button. A ‘remove’ button was also added to each child input row to allow parents to reduce the number of children on the subscription without leaving the page.

High-Fidelity designs

When moving into the high-fidelity stages of the design, I worked alongside the design lead to create a design system for this project and future B2C use cases to ensure consistency and accessibility.

This process included tokenising elements of the design. Together we built out a colour palette, defined a type scale, built out a button library and defined custom components for future use.

Where possible, we worked to utilise existing components within the Chakra Design System framework, styling them to fit consistently, enabling us to reduce the complexity for implementation.

Creating visual delight & balance

I added subtle background elements into the design to create a sense of flow and guide the users down the page. This also helped to add warmth to the page that the stark white pages were missing.

I created a small animation on the pricing page for the call to action. This animates when the user scrolls the image's container into view, drawing the user's eye to this call to action button.

To create a final moment of delight the end of the purchase flow, I added a short confetti burst to the page.

Results & Next Steps

Relaunch

The updated user interface was implemented by the development team before the application was relaunched with iOS and Android native applications. Upon relaunch, the application saw a 50% increase in users over the first month.

Next steps

Further user testing should be conducted to analyse the user flows to validate the designs and provide recommendations for future iterations.

The micro-copy and labels on the application, especially surrounding the 'offers to help' and 'offers of help' should also be validated in focus groups to ensure all functionality is easy to navigate and understand.
